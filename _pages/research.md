**Gender Role Attitudes and Female Labour Force Participation: Empirical Evidence from European Countries**
<br>with Franziska Gassmann (UNU-MERIT and Maastricht University)

<details style="margin: 0.3rem 0 1.2rem;">
<summary style="display: list-item; cursor: pointer; color: var(--global-theme-color);">Abstract</summary>
<p style="margin-top: 0.5rem;">Drawing on identity economics, this paper examines whether gender role attitudes shape women's labour force participation across 27 European countries. Using three waves of the European Values Study (1990--2010), we construct a gender role attitudes index through factor analysis and estimate linear probability and two-stage least squares models, instrumenting attitudes with beliefs about casual sex. We find that a one-standard-deviation increase in traditional gender role attitudes reduces women's probability of participating in the labour force by approximately 8 percentage points, while the corresponding effect among men is negligible. Attitudes towards dual-earner households, housewifery and maternal employment are most strongly associated with participation. The relationship is more pronounced among married women and mothers, and weaker among women in low-income households despite their more traditional gender role attitudes. Traditional attitudes among women of the same country and birth cohort are negatively associated with women's decision to join the labour force. However, this peer effect disappears once women's own gender role attitudes are accounted for, suggesting that peer attitudes may partly operate through individual attitudes. By examining both individual and peer attitudes across European countries and over time, this paper provides new evidence on how social and cultural factors relate to women's economic decisions and, ultimately, gender convergence in labour market participation.</p>
